# HO Victorian buildings: the owner's rules

These are the owner's own directions and fixes, collected from the whole project. Follow them on
every building. Construction standards are in `hoarch/NOTES.md`, and the building list and
uniqueness tables are in `hoarch/COLLECTION.md`.

## Product
- The models are sold printed and finished (eBay; HO houses have sold for $100+ plus shipping).
  They have to be really good. Nothing bland, easy or "anyone can make it". No typical
  AI-model look.
- Scale is HO (1:87.1). There are no lights inside and no removable roof, for now.
- The owner paints and colours the models. Label the files with the colours in the render.
- The colour schemes so far are liked. Keep that approach.

## Printing
- Printers: Bambu Lab H2C, P2S and P1S (the P2S and P1S have a 256 x 256 bed), and a
  Snapmaker Artisan. Layers are 0.20 mm preferred, 0.16 mm at the finest.
- A house should fill the P1S/P2S bed. Make buildings big, not skinny.
- Plates:
  - Single-colour plates, so a part prints on any printer.
  - A part may be two colours, as long as that is only one colour change (a filament change at
    one layer height). Waste is negligible. The Ashby Villa windows were done this way.
  - Single-colour parts are fine too when they're easy.
  - Count colour changes per part, not colours.
- Supports:
  - None, except under window and door frames.
  - No support squiggles inside eaves or under trim.
- Window glass needs at least 2 layers; one layer lets light through.
- Porch posts are round, not boxy, and printed upright. A post printed on its side comes out
  glossy on one face and boxy on the other.

## Construction (like the pink house)
- Walls: one part for the whole first floor, one part for the whole second floor.
- Use stacking slices. Put layered trim between the main parts (floors, roof, porch):
  multiple eave and ornament layers.
- Cornices:
  - A cornice between every level, including under the roof.
  - Each cornice is built from several parts in several colours.
  - Never the same design on two buildings. It should look expert, not generic.
  - Double eaves are welcome for more detail.
- Porch floors are slotted boards (planks), with extra detail (blocks and so on) like the pink
  house.
- Windows and doors:
  - Frame and glass/inlay are one part (a plug plus a frame), with supports under the frame.
  - Frames must fit into the window openings.
  - Windows must not fill a storey's whole height or a wall's whole width.

## Style and uniqueness
- Every building is unique. Never reuse wall skins, porch parts, trim, cornices or anything
  else between buildings.
- Wall skins:
  - Prefer lined paneling (lap siding).
  - No "U"-shaped (fish-scale) shingle panels.
  - Offer more brick options.
- Window frames and doors are where the ornament goes to the next level:
  - Flowing, not boxy.
  - Upper and lower frames differ.
  - Doors and door frames extra ornate and unique.
- Detail has to be unique and cool, not the easy way.
- No white picket fences.
- Towers and cupolas:
  - Big enough that their windows aren't tiny.
  - Not too many windows (a tower top was "too many windows").
  - A porch round a tower is liked.

## Fixes already asked for (don't regress them)
- The Ashby floors were 35-40% smaller than the pink house. All houses were too skinny and
  are now the house-size standard.
- Rosecroft:
  - Widen it and give it a bigger cupola.
  - Put a porch round the tower.
  - Crown: fewer windows, with visible frames.
- Larkspur:
  - The round dormer turret was too small and weird; it is now a gabled dormer.
  - The gable window must not be covered.
- Camellia: fewer tower windows; the gable window must not be covered.
- Marigold: no bicycle.
- Wisteria: fewer porch posts.

## Working with the owner
- Show a render before going all in; the owner reviews renders and asks for revisions.
- Include a rear view in every building's renders, so parts at the back (a kitchen ell's
  three-sided cornice, say) have a picture to match against.
- Provide finished print files as zips.
- Get a few pilot buildings right before rolling a change across the collection. Don't pile
  fix on fix across every model at once.
- Mind usage limits and keep working between them.
