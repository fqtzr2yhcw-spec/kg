# perCVus — funding application (draft)

**One line:** perCVus is a debate platform where an AI referee scores the *quality of your
reasoning* — not your side — and turns the argument into a living map of what's actually
been established. A gym for thinking, and a machine for getting closer to the truth.

## The problem
The internet optimized discourse for the wrong thing. Feeds reward outrage and volume, so
being loud beats being right, and the person who is *sincerely wrong* — the raw material
of figuring anything out — gets shouted down instead of corrected. Worse, nothing is ever
*settled*: a million-comment argument leaves no record of what both sides came to accept,
what's genuinely still in dispute, or what evidence would resolve it. Most disagreement
isn't even about facts — it's about how differently people *perceive* the same thing. We
have no machinery for improving that.

## The idea
perCVus makes one move: it puts a neutral **AI referee** between the two sides. Every
contribution is scored on independent axes — **rigor, novelty, and bridge-building** —
each with a published, contestable reason. Civility is a gate, not a scoreboard: you can
be as sharp or as wrong as you like, but you can't out-argue an insult, and nonsense earns
nothing. Good reasoning earns *floor-time* and standing; it never takes away anyone's
right to speak. And continuously, the referee publishes the **State of the Floor** — a
synthesis of what's established, what's contested, and what would resolve it. Over time
those syntheses compound into a **tree of propositions**: a growing corpus of collective
understanding. It's the anti-feed.

## Why now
The neutral referee — something that can read an argument and judge whether the premises
actually support the conclusion — was science fiction three years ago. Now it's a prompt.
Every previous attempt at "better online debate" had to fake the referee with rules,
karma, or moderators, and it never held. perCVus is what becomes buildable the moment the
referee is real. I've already written the referee's full scoring rubric and a stress-test
battery to prove it's right *before* spending a dollar on scale.

## Why me

perCVus is named for me. My friend **Jerry Reynolds** gave me the name — because I couldn't
get through an argument without insisting that *perception is 9/10ths of understanding*. We
lived in one-bedroom apartments next door to each other off the UTSA campus in San Antonio:
two people hungry for knowledge and honesty, spending long nights daring each other to
prove our positions, often with real psychology experiments. One night Jerry bet me that if
I stood at a bus stop in a bedsheet toga, no one would ask why — and that no stranger would
ever write down their name and number to take part in a "study." I told him curiosity would
win before the third stop, as long as I never spoke first, and as long as I made whoever
asked feel like they'd *won* — *you're curious enough to engage; you win!* — and then gave
them as much of the real story as they wanted. Someone did. That bet is the whole thesis of
perCVus: with the right framing, people *want* to engage honestly, and **how you say it
matters as much as what you say**. Jerry and I made each other sharper by testing each
other's assumptions — perCVus is that friendship turned into something strangers can share.
He died about five years after we left school, and I still feel the gap where his birthday
call used to be. I've worn the name he gave me ever since. This is in his honor.

## What I'll do with the money (6 months, concrete)
1. **Validate the referee** — run the stress-test battery through the model against the
   rubric; confirm scores are right, stable, and unbiased on charged topics. (~free.)
2. **Run one real floor** — an invite-only founding cohort of 30–50 thoughtful people on a
   handful of live motions, with the real referee scoring in the loop.
3. **Measure the one thing that matters** — do they come back? Retention on a single
   living floor is the evidence that decides everything downstream.
4. **Publish openly** — the rubric, the results, what worked and what didn't.

## The ask
**$[20,000].** It covers the model/hosting costs for the pilot, a modest stipend so I can
build this close to full-time for six months, and seeding the founding community. This
isn't a raise for a big team — it's the smallest amount that turns a thought-out concept
into a real, tested thing with real people on it.

## The vision if it works
A reputation layer for good reasoning. A public record of what humanity has actually
argued its way to. A place where the sincere-but-unskilled are welcomed and *taught*
rather than shouted down. Not another platform for winning — a commons for understanding.

*I'm building this because the world is worse off without people like Jerry in it — and
better with more of the honest, curious argument we had. I want to give that to strangers.*
